## Video direction

- Palette system: warm paper `bg-primary` carries every frame; ink `text-primary` is the dominant branch and display copy; `text-secondary` supports; Quillium purple `accent`/`line` appears only when abstract branching becomes a product revision. One-pixel hairlines are the shared visual atom.
- Typography: Newsreader display copy feels literary and human; Inter handles labels and product-supporting copy. Display remains sentence case and light in weight.
- Motion grammar: one continuous lineage—SVG self-draw (`svg-path-draw`) → branch expansion (`center-outward-expansion`) → targeted camera push (`coordinate-target-zoom`) → UI surface → branch-to-mark lockup. Use smooth long-tail settles, never bounce. With no voiceover, reveals follow the four musical accents and continue across each shot's back half.
- Rhythm: Frame 1 is the long crescendo; Frame 2 resolves into clarity; Frames 3–4 deliver proof; Frame 5 is the deliberate breather. Inter-scene reading holds run 1.4–1.8 seconds—one second shorter than the previous cut—while Frame 2 spends that reclaimed second on its branching-to-revision transformation. Transitions keep their long, soft overlaps.
- Portrait composition: proof surfaces occupy at least 70% of the safe width and use stacked crops or vertical camera travel. Never shrink a landscape desktop screenshot into the middle of the portrait canvas. Essential controls and highlighted prose must read at phone size.
- Safe composition: essential copy and UI stay within the top 83% and x=70–940; branches may bleed beyond the canvas. Maintain one primary focal point and at most one secondary line of copy.
- Never: generic particles, neon gradients, dashboard chrome, floating independent objects, rounded SaaS-card stacks, bouncy springs, lazy breathing, back-half camera drift, front-loaded slideshow motion, or screensaver motion.
