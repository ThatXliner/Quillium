# Quillium Marketing Strategy

## Positioning

Quillium is not a replacement for Google Docs — it's a better place to *think and write* before you format and share.

> "Write in Quillium. Polish in Google Docs."

The constraint is the identity. Lead with what Quillium is *not*:

> "No autocomplete. No AI ghostwriting. No formatting toolbar. Just your words and a better way to revise them."

This is a selling point, not a disclaimer — AI-averse writers will feel seen immediately.

## What's Actually Differentiating

- **Non-linear editing / paragraph branching** — version control for prose, without the Git UX nightmare
- **Revision as exploration** — not destructive overwriting
- **Annotations and comments** — structured reflection within the writing itself
- **No formatting distraction** — the constraint enforces focus

## Target Audience

Serious longform writers who think carefully about revision as craft. Specifically:

- Deep-novel / literary fiction writers
- AI-skeptical writers who left Twitter for Bluesky
- Craft-focused newsletter/Substack writers (not grifters)

Avoid: developers, productivity hackers, AI enthusiasts — these audiences will misread the product.

## The Category Perception Problem

"New writing tool" pattern-matches to "AI slop generator" in 2025. Messaging must break this assumption fast. The blog posts at [quillium.bryanhu.com](https://quillium.bryanhu.com) already do this well — the gap is distribution, not positioning.

## Distribution Strategy

### Stage 1: Manual seeding (now, pre-scale)

Don't look for a scalable channel yet. Find 5–10 writers manually and get them genuinely using it. Social proof and word-of-mouth have to exist before channels work.

**How:**
- Search Bluesky/Twitter for writers publicly complaining about revision tools ("I hate how Word handles drafts", "wish there was a better way to manage revisions")
- Engage genuinely first, pitch later
- Post about *the problem* in writing communities, not the product — let people respond and discover Quillium naturally

### Stage 2: Community presence

**Bluesky** is the highest-priority channel:
- Writing community there is active and craft-focused
- Skews AI-skeptical — your "no AI ghostwriting" message lands without effort
- Many writers migrated there specifically due to AI content concerns on Twitter

**Reddit:**
- r/writing (most active general writing community)
- r/nanowrimo (NaNoWriMo org collapsed after 2023 controversy, but community still active here)
- r/worldbuilding

Note: NaNoWriMo org is largely dead but refugees scattered to Discord servers and the subreddit.

### Stage 3: Content

The blog is already doing the right thing. Keep writing in that voice — craft-focused, honest about what Quillium is and isn't. This builds trust with the exact audience you want.

## What to Avoid

- **Product Hunt** — audience skews toward AI tool enthusiasts, creates exactly the wrong first impression
- **Hacker News** — skews developer/analytical, not writers; posts have historically flopped (~6 upvotes)
- **Leading with AI** even to disclaim it — don't put it in the headline
